## Session Summary

Section 91 of the Constitution Act, 1867 grants the federal Parliament legislative authority over specific matters, forming a cornerstone of Canadian constitutional law. It defines the scope of federal powers and, together with Section 92 which outlines provincial powers, establishes the division of powers fundamental to Canadian federalism. Understanding Section 91 is essential for grasping how federal and provincial jurisdictions interact, including key doctrines such as POGG, paramountcy, and double aspect.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Section 91 specifies the legislative powers of the federal Parliament in Canada.
2. Section 91 works in tandem with Section 92 to delineate federal and provincial jurisdictions.
3. The division of powers is central to Canadian federalism and constitutional law.
4. Doctrines like POGG, paramountcy, and double aspect help interpret the relationship between federal and provincial powers.
